UCI M.Eng Capstone

Sustainable 3D Printing

Material research project advancing eco-friendly 3D-printable concrete mixtures for sustainable construction practices

Project Overview

This capstone project for the University of California, Irvine Master of Engineering Program was sponsored by Gaudi Technology, a 3D printing construction company driven by the use of renewables in their concrete mixtures. This was a material research-based project to develop 3D-printable concrete mixtures using different recycled materials to advance eco-friendly and sustainable printing practices.

Following the devastating Los Angeles fires in early 2025, our team and Gaudi Technology identified an opportunity to support rebuilding efforts through innovative 3D-printed construction practices. This project focused on advancing the construction industry by exploring eco-friendly material innovations in 3D concrete printing.

The work evaluated the compressive and tensile strength of studied eco-friendly and recyclable alternative materials while being incorporated into Gaudi Tech's proprietary concrete, Gaudicrete. The main objective was to demonstrate sustainable solutions that maintain structural integrity with minimal performance trade-offs.

Project Goals

  • Develop multiple concrete mix designs based on independent material research
  • Ensure all mixes meet minimum requirements defined by ACI 318 and the International Building Code (IBC) for structural concrete
  • Produce testing reports for each mix, including comparative analyses against Gaudi Tech's baseline Gaudicrete data

My Focus

Key Finding: RCP and Gypsum Mixture

My focus was on the RCP and Gypsum mixture, created by Gaudi Technology as a special study. This mixture consisted of:

  • 20% RCP (Recycled Concrete Product)
  • 20% Gypsum
  • 20% Water
  • 40% Gaudicrete
Result: This mixture came out as the overall best, achieving a 4% higher compressive strength compared to a normal Gaudicrete mixture.

CAD Modeling: The e+ Pedestal

The team wanted to leave a lasting memory of this capstone project considering how important this data is to the city of Los Angeles, while Gaudi Technology wanted to make a lasting impression on the University and the city of Irvine. This collaboration resulted in the decision to donate a pedestal to the University.

The e+ Pedestal takes inspiration from the Samueli e+ logo and is planned to sit in UCI's Engineering Gateway to serve as a beacon of inspiration to all students. The pedestal will be printed with Gaudi Technology's proprietary Gaudicrete mixture.
